About me

I'm Ulli, a theoretical and computational astrophysicist. I write simulation codes, run them on some of the largest supercomputers available, and use them to ask how galaxies regulate themselves.

Most of my work is about feedback and the multiphase gas it creates: how individual supernovae drive hot, warm and cold winds out of galaxies, how those winds carry mass, energy and metals, and how the same physics plays out in the plasma of galaxy clusters. Magnetic fields run through all of it, and much of my early work showed how turbulent dynamos grow them in galaxies and clusters.

I'm also a code developer, and my two flagship projects are codes. Stella is my star-by-star stellar feedback model in GIZMO: it samples individual stars, gives each one its own stellar evolution, and resolves every supernova at about one solar mass resolution. Luna is my own GPU-native, end-to-end differentiable hydrodynamics code, so that simulations can be fitted, inverted and optimised like any other model.

Before moving to the MPA in 2025, I was a Flatiron Research Fellow at the Center for Computational Astrophysics in New York. I did my PhD at LMU Munich and studied physics in Konstanz.

Research

Four threads, one theme: resolving the small-scale physics that decides what happens on large scales.

Flagship · Stella

Multiphase galactic winds, star by star

With Stella, every star in a galaxy is followed individually, with its own stellar evolution, winds, radiation and supernova. At about one solar mass resolution I can watch the hot, warm and cold phases of a wind being launched, and measure how much mass, energy and metals each one carries. The image shows a WLM-mass dwarf galaxy whose interstellar medium has been carved into bubbles by individual supernovae.

I use these simulations, from dwarf galaxies to the LMC and tall-box patches of denser discs, to test how winds depend on the variety of supernova outcomes, on runaway stars and on turbulent metal mixing, and to build analytic wind models that observers can use.

  • Pumping Iron: turbulent metal diffusion and multiphase outflows ApJ 2025
  • Some Stars Fade Quietly: varied supernova outcomes and the ISM ApJ 2025
  • Multiphase winds in an LMC-mass galaxy ApJ 2024

Galaxy clusters and the Coma cluster

Starting from initial conditions constrained by the observed local Universe, I simulate fifty possible histories of the Coma cluster. Their X-ray and Sunyaev-Zel'dovich profiles match eROSITA and Planck, and the spread between them shows how much of what we see is cosmic variance. I also push cluster simulations to the scale where Coulomb collisions in the plasma become resolved.

  • Learning the Universe: constrained simulations of the Coma cluster I 2026
  • Towards the magnetized ICM with resolved Coulomb collision scale ApJ 2024

Magnetic fields and turbulence

Galaxies and clusters are magnetised, and turbulence is how they get there. My simulations showed the small-scale turbulent dynamo at work in Milky Way-like galaxies and in the intracluster medium, how it hands over to a large-scale galactic dynamo, and how strong central fields can help launch outflows.

  • The small-scale turbulent dynamo in the ICM ApJ 2022
  • Driving galactic outflows with magnetic fields ApJ 2022
  • Magnetic winds and the galactic dynamo MNRAS 2020

Flagship · Luna

Differentiable hydrodynamics on GPUs

Luna is my own fluid code written in JAX that is differentiable from end to end: hydrodynamics, self-gravity, collisionless particles and radiative cooling. Because every step has a gradient, you can ask the simulation questions backwards, for example which initial conditions produce a given final state. Above: the initial energy and velocity fields were optimised so that the flow spells out its own name.

Luna runs natively on GPUs and scales across many of them, supports adaptive mesh refinement, and is tested against established codes such as AthenaK and Arepo, from shock tubes to the cosmic web.

In development; first results presented at the Astronomische Gesellschaft meeting 2026.
